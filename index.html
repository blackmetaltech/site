I · AI infrastructure

GIGAWATT

Build an AI datacenter, 2020 to 2028.

Alpha 6 · v0.9.5

Buy real GPUs, NICs and switches, design your own server trays and racks, power and cool a hall, wire a fat-tree fabric into a supercomputer, and lease it to customers who want to train and serve frontier models. Meanwhile electricity prices, the hardware roadmap and the odd backhoe conspire against you.

What you learn

  • Power and cooling, from transformer and UPS to chillers and CDUs
  • Network fabrics: fat-trees, bisection, what a cluster can train
  • MFU, tokens per second and time to first token
  • Contracts, loans, staffing and hardware lifecycles

Linux · Windows · macOS · Android (preview). Mouse, controller or touch.

II · Neuroscience

SPIKE

Wire neurons, run a body.

Alpha · v0.5.0

Build small spiking neural circuits and watch them drive a body: a leg kicks, a lamprey swims, a heart speeds up when you stand, a worm backs away from a touch. Every neuron is a real NEST model simulated live, in NEST's own units, drawn like a 19th-century histology plate.

What you learn

  • How neurons fire, from integrate-and-fire to Hodgkin-Huxley
  • Reflexes, rhythm generators, the heart, breathing and the gut
  • Diagnosis: find the hidden lesion in a patient's circuit
  • Phase planes, attractor memory and population decoding

Desktop Linux. 79 levels in three wings. No binary release is published yet: build it from source.

III · Power engineering

GRID

Keep the lights on with real physics.

Alpha · v1.13.0

Build a province's power grid one job at a time, then press Run. Demand climbs at dusk, the sun sets on the solar parks, a storm opens a line, a big unit trips. Towns go dark window by window, and the windows are lit by your own power flow.

What you learn

  • Ohm's law up to a Newton-Raphson AC load flow
  • Voltage control, harmonics and three-phase balance
  • Frequency and droop, relay curves and fault levels
  • Merit-order dispatch and a ten-year utility career

Linux · Windows (built and smoke-tested, not yet played on Windows). 36 levels and a finale. Mouse, controller or touch.

Textbooks describe. Games answer back.

Real engines

Each game runs the real model: a Newton-Raphson load flow in GRID, NEST neurons in SPIKE, a deterministic datacenter engine in GIGAWATT. If the physics says no, the game says no.

Failure is the lesson

Get it wrong and the game tells you what broke and why, never just the answer. Then you fix it and run it again.

Forged with AI, played by humans

AI agents work the forge so a small house can build games this deep. The point is the opposite of outsourcing your thinking: you learn the machine by running it.

The AI age rewards people who understand systems. A textbook cannot hand you a grid to break. We can.
